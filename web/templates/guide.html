How Delvefall works

Delvefall is a shortcut. It isn't meant to replace Scryfall: it's a tool for bypassing the tags, and you don't need to know Scryfall syntax to use it. It isn't a chatbot guessing either: it compares the printed rules text of every Magic card with plain math. That math can be reversed to get the most unique cards in Magic.

Two kinds of similar, counted together

Every result is scored twice, and the two scores are averaged.

  • rules text: cards whose printed text works the same way. Search Sol Ring, get the other rocks that tap for two.
  • concepts: cards built around the same idea, from community tags. Search Sol Ring, get the whole mana-rock family.
  • The badge on every card is the average of the two, and the list is always in order of the number you see. Hover it to break the number back into its halves.

Weaker matches aren't thrown away. The button under the results opens them ten percentage points at a time and labels each batch, which is what keeps the sorts honest: sorting one band by price answers "what is the cheapest card that matches this well", where opening everything below the line at once would just hand you the cheapest card in the game with a faint resemblance. Anything that reloads the page, the sort included, starts you back at the strong matches.

Filters

The whole vocabulary:

  • o:word rules text contains it, o:"draw a card" for phrases
  • t:legendary the type line must contain this, any word works
  • id:wu colour identity (fits-within), id:c colourless
  • otag:removal the community tags, served from this site's own daily mirror of scryfall's tagger data
  • usd<5 eur>=1 gbp<2 price, price<5 whichever currency the switch shows, mv<=2 cmc=4 mana value
  • salt>=1.5 how much players dislike facing a card, from EDHREC's salt survey. Runs 0 to about 3, and only 2% of cards pass 1. Cards nobody voted on have no score and fail any salt comparison
  • is:dfc two-sided cards, is:split is:mdfc is:saga and other layouts, is:gamechanger the commander watchlist
  • f:commander legal in commander, banned:commander the reverse (commander is the only format tracked)
  • a - negates anything (-t:creature means no creatures), words side by side mean and, or and parentheses group: (o:draw or o:scry) -t:creature

That's the whole vocabulary, not all of scryfall's. There's no pow/tou, rarity, set, artist, format beyond commander, or regex here yet, the cards table just doesn't carry those fields. Anything the box doesn't recognise is skipped rather than erroring, so a stray word never breaks the search.

Where the pound price comes from

Scryfall quotes dollars and euros only, so pounds are worked out here rather than looked up: both prices are converted at the day's ECB rates and averaged, or whichever one the card has if it only has one. A card with neither has no pound price, and any price filter hides it.

So a pound figure is a fair estimate, not a price anyone is actually asking. For a real number, check a UK seller. Dollars and euros are printed exactly as scryfall reports them.

Unique cards

The unique page runs the same math backwards: a card's score is one minus its best match anywhere in Magic, on both axes and averaged the same way a search is. A card at 90% has nothing out there more than 10% like it.

Three things about it you can't see from the page:

  • It is a draw, not a running order. It finds the best card left and then deals at random from everything within 5% of it, so two people opening the page do not walk the same sequence, and every press still comes off the top of what remains.
  • It never deals you the same card twice, and the record of what you have been dealt lives in your browser, so it survives closing the tab and picks up months later. Clearing it is what makes the whole game fresh again.
  • A card with no rules text the matcher can read is never dealt at all. A vanilla creature is not unique, it has nothing to be unique about.

The deck lens

Paste a decklist or import one from Archidekt or Moxfield and the same reading gets applied to a whole deck: how original it is, how salty, what it costs, how played its cards are and how old, each one placed against every Commander precon. Placing a deck needs at least {{ deck_min }} nonland cards. Shorter lists are still read, they just aren't ranked.

Change it walks the deck a card at a time, worst first, and offers replacements. Cards with nothing close enough are stepped over rather than swapped for something worse, and they're named together at the end, because "nothing else does this" is a real finding about a card.

Saved decks live in your browser and nowhere else. Nothing about a deck reaches the server beyond the request that reads it, which is why there's no account to make and why your decks aren't waiting for you on another machine. Reopening a deck you have swapped picks up where you left it, and the list it arrived as is kept alongside.

Found a bad match, or missing a card you expected? The report link on any results page is the fastest way to change what happens next: those reports become the test cases the matcher has to pass.

Rules you can't see

The site holds cards back on purpose in a few places. None of these are failures, but all of them look like one if you don't know they're there.

  • A replacement has to cost about the same. Similarity is measured on rules text, and rules text can't see a curve: a two mana rock and a six mana rock read identically on the ability that makes them rocks. So Change it only offers cards near the mana value of the card leaving, within 2 up to five mana and within 3 past it. Above eight it stops counting distance and simply reaches down to five, because up there everything is a finisher. This is why a search can show you fourteen cards and the swap tool offer one: the page now says how many it held back and what the window was.
  • A replacement has to share a real line of text. Cards that match only on community tags are never offered for a slot, however well they score, and everything offered has to clear 75%.
  • It searches the card's rarest ability, not its best-matching one. Stasis and Sunken City match perfectly on their upkeep tax, and the untap lock, the entire reason anybody plays or hates Stasis, would go unexamined. So a swap anchors on what makes a card that card, which is sometimes not the line you expected it to use.
  • A missing number fails every comparison. A card nobody has priced is hidden by any price filter, and a card nobody voted on in the salt survey is hidden by any salt filter. Neither is treated as a zero, because a card with no salt score is not a card players don't mind facing.
  • Lands are left out of a deck's originality and age, on both your deck and every precon it is placed against. A mana base is not what makes a deck a deck. A deck also needs at least {{ deck_min }} nonland cards before it can be placed at all; shorter lists are still read, just not ranked.
  • Nothing about a deck reaches the server beyond the request that reads it. Saved decks, swap sessions and the unique page's history are all in your browser, which is why there is no account and why none of it follows you to another machine.
