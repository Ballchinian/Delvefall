{% if sort.metric.key == 'original' %}

It is not measuring how good or how expensive a deck is. Against the decks' median EDHREC rank the correlation is r = -0.05, which is nothing. Original and popular turn out to be unrelated, and both are on this board as separate rankings for exactly that reason.

It is partly measuring how recent a deck is (r = +0.46 against release year). Some of that is real: design space fills up, so a card from 2011 has had fifteen years of imitators, and being imitated is the opposite of being unique. The ranking still separates decks inside a single era, which is what the era buttons are for.

Read from the top HALF of each deck's nonland cards, a share of each deck rather than a fixed number, so a deck holding 53 nonland cards and one holding 66 are judged on the same slice of themselves. Half rather than a third because a third of a commander deck is mostly the mana base's neighbours and the staples every deck brings, and reading deeper than half only pulls every deck toward the same middle.

{% elif sort.metric.key == 'salt' %}

Basic lands are left out, and nothing else is. Not because the votes on them are wrong, but because counting them measures how a mana base was built rather than how a deck plays: counting one of each charged five colour decks for being five colours, counting every copy charged mono decks for running thirty Islands. The distortion flipped direction with the choice, which is how you know it belonged to the arithmetic and not to the decks. Every other land counts, including Gaea's Cradle and The Tabernacle at Pendrell Vale.

Protest votes count. The scores are used exactly as voted. Filtering the ones that look unserious would mean overriding the poll with our own taste, and then it would stop measuring what it says it measures.

It is an opinion, not a measurement. Everything else on this board is derived from what a card does or costs. This one is how a table feels about facing it, from EDHREC's salt survey, and no amount of reading rules text produces it.

{% elif sort.metric.key == 'price' %}

Basic lands DO count here, unlike the salt tally, and the two are different kinds of number: salt is an opinion about a card and nine Islands are not nine times the annoyance, where price is money and a deck's cost includes the lands you have to own to play it.

Prices are the cheapest paper printing in any finish, refreshed from Scryfall daily. Digital printings, oversized promos and gold border world championship cards don't count, since you can't sleeve those up.

Euro totals read low. Scryfall's euro coverage is thinner than its dollar coverage, and an unpriced card silently contributes nothing to a sum. The ranking survives, because every deck is summed the same way and drops the same cards, but the absolute euro figure understates what a deck is worth. Dollars are the honest column.

{% elif sort.metric.key == 'played' %}

The median, not the mean. EDHREC's rank is ordinal over the whole format, so one card sitting at rank 31,000 drags a mean somewhere no card in the deck actually is. The median says what a typical card in this deck is, which is the question.

Lands are left out, matching the originality convention: every deck runs Command Tower and it says nothing about which deck this is.

It is not a measure of power. Played and original are unrelated by our own measurement (r = -0.05), and neither of them is strength. A deck of staples is a deck of cards other people also bought.

{% elif sort.metric.key == 'age' %}

Measured from the FIRST printing, so a reprint does not make an old card new. A deck published last year and built from reprints reads old here, which is the point: the question is how old the cards are, not how old the product is.

Basic lands are left out, same as the salt tally and for the same reason: thirty Islands at three decades each is around 900 years that say nothing about a deck.

Ranked on the average, printed with the total. The total is the memorable fact and the average is the only one a ranking can honestly use, because precons hold anywhere from 53 to 66 nonland cards and a sum quietly rewards the bigger deck for being bigger.
