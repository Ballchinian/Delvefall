The saltiest Commander precons

Every preconstructed Commander deck, ranked by how much its cards annoy people. This is the one ranking here built on opinion rather than measurement: it comes from EDHREC's salt survey, where players vote on the cards they least enjoy facing.

The most original Commander precons

Every preconstructed Commander deck, ranked by how unusual its cards are. Not how strong, not how expensive, and not how popular: how little the rest of Magic looks like it. A card scores high here when nothing else in the game does what it does.

How this is measured, and what it doesn't measure {% if salty %}

Every card carries a salt score from EDHREC's annual survey, 0 to about 3. A deck's score is the sum across its cards, so a deck is salty by holding many mildly annoying cards or a few very annoying ones.

Basic lands are left out, and nothing else is. Not because the votes on them are wrong, but because counting them measures how a mana base was built rather than how a deck plays: counting one of each charged five colour decks for being five colours, counting every copy charged mono decks for running thirty Islands. The distortion flipped direction with the choice, which is how you know it belonged to the arithmetic and not to the decks. Every other land counts, including Gaea's Cradle and The Tabernacle at Pendrell Vale.

Protest votes count. The scores are used exactly as voted. Filtering the ones that look unserious would mean overriding the poll with our own taste, and then it would stop measuring what it says it measures.

{% else %}

Every card carries an originality score: one minus how close its closest match anywhere else in Magic gets. A deck's score is the average of its most original {{ depth_label|lower }} of nonland cards — a share of each deck rather than a fixed number, so a deck holding 53 nonland cards and one holding 66 are each judged on the same slice of themselves.

Reading deeper does not make it more accurate. Precons differ mostly in their unusual cards; the rest is the mana base and the removal package everyone shares, so adding them pulls every deck toward the same middle. The spread between first and last falls from 0.143 at a third to 0.103 using every card. The ends of the board hardly move at all — the same deck is first, and the same five are last, at every setting.

It is not measuring how good or how expensive a deck is. Against the decks' median EDHREC rank the correlation is r = -0.05, which is nothing. Original and popular turn out to be unrelated.

It is partly measuring how recent a deck is (r = +0.46 against release year). Some of that is real: design space fills up, so a card from 2011 has had fifteen years of imitators, and being imitated is the opposite of being unique. The ranking still separates decks inside a single era, which is what the era buttons are for.
